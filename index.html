01 — THE PROBLEM

Why renewable-energy portfolio selection is an optimization problem.

Renewable energy generation potential varies significantly across geographic regions due to differing solar irradiance and wind patterns. For this study, we consider five key Indian states: Rajasthan, Gujarat, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, and Andhra Pradesh.

Given a constrained budget, the core objective is to select exactly TWO regions to fund, maximizing the combined annual generation yield (measured in Terawatt-hours, TWh).

Research Question

"Can QAOA identify the optimal renewable-energy portfolio while satisfying the strict selection constraint?"

Why Optimization Is Needed

For N=5 regions picking k=2, the total combinations C(5,2) = 10 is trivial to brute-force classically. However, this research explores formulation and algorithmic scaling. As N grows (e.g., micro-grids across hundreds of districts), the combinatorial space explodes. We investigate if the QUBO formulation and QAOA solver can efficiently handle this constrained search space.

Decision Variables

We introduce x₁ to x₅ as binary variables where 0 = not selected and 1 = selected.

Exactly two regions must be selected.

QUBO Formulation

Quantum computers solve optimization problems by finding the ground state of a Hamiltonian. We must convert our constrained problem into a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) format.

Cost Function C(x)
C(x) = - Σ Eixi + A ( Σ xi - 2 )2
trending_up Objective Term

Minimizing negative yield is equivalent to maximizing yield. Eᵢ is the total energy for region i.

gavel Penalty Term

Enforces the constraint. If sum(xᵢ) ≠ 2, a massive penalty (A=50) is added, raising the energy state far above the ground state.

Future Scope & Scalability

While N=5 is trivial classically, mapping real-world grids involves thousands of variables (micro-grids, dynamic pricing, storage limits). The state space grows as 2ᴺ.

Limitations

  • NISQ Era Noise: Real quantum hardware introduces decoherence. Our results are from a noise-free simulator.
  • No Quantum Advantage: For p=2, QAOA on dense MAXCUT-like QUBOs does not currently guarantee supremacy over advanced classical heuristics (e.g., Simulated Annealing).
  • Penalty Scaling: Large penalty coefficients (A) warp the energy landscape, making optimization harder for the classical COBYLA loop within QAOA.
