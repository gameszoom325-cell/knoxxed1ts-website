Quantum Optimization for Renewable Energy Portfolios

Formulating a high-yield renewable energy selection problem as a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) model and simulating solving it via the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA).

01

Problem

Given limited capital, how do we select a subset of renewable energy regions to maximize total energy yield (Solar + Wind) while strictly adhering to budget constraints.

02

Objective

Maximize Σ Eᵢxᵢ where Eᵢ is energy yield and xᵢ ∈ {0,1}. Constraint: Select exactly k=2 regions.

03

Approach

Map the constrained optimization problem to a QUBO formulation, map variables to qubits, and simulate solving via QAOA with depth p=2.

04

Evaluation

Compare state probabilities output by the quantum circuit against the classical brute-force baseline to measure optimization performance.

QUBO Formulation

Quantum computers solve optimization problems by finding the ground state of a Hamiltonian. We must convert our constrained problem into a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) format.

Cost Function C(x)
C(x) = - Σ Eixi + A ( Σ xi - 2 )2
trending_up Objective Term

Minimizing negative yield is equivalent to maximizing yield. Eᵢ is the total energy for region i.

gavel Penalty Term

Enforces the constraint. If sum(xᵢ) ≠ 2, a massive penalty (A=50) is added, raising the energy state far above the ground state.

Future Scope & Scalability

While N=5 is trivial classically, mapping real-world grids involves thousands of variables (micro-grids, dynamic pricing, storage limits). The state space grows as 2ᴺ.

Limitations

  • NISQ Era Noise: Real quantum hardware introduces decoherence. Our results are from a noise-free simulator.
  • No Quantum Advantage: For p=2, QAOA on dense MAXCUT-like QUBOs does not currently guarantee supremacy over advanced classical heuristics (e.g., Simulated Annealing).
  • Penalty Scaling: Large penalty coefficients (A) warp the energy landscape, making optimization harder for the classical COBYLA loop within QAOA.
